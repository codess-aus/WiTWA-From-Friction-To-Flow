# WiTWA-From-Friction-To-Flow

From Friction to Flow
The Confidence to Build What's Next
Format: 25-minute inspirational keynote Audience: Technical and non-technical Women in Tech WA attendees Approach: Story-driven, no live demo; use a simple visual walkthrough of the agentic SDLC.

Narrative arc
Friction -> Confidence -> AI as a bridge -> Agentic flow -> Human judgment -> Action

Slide sequence and talk track
1. You do not have to feel ready to build what's next
Time: 1 minute

Slide copy:

You do not have to feel ready to build what's next.

Talk track:
“Today is not a talk about becoming a programmer in 25 minutes. It is a talk about what happens when the distance between an idea and a first useful version gets smaller. We will explore GitHub AI and the agentic software development lifecycle, but underneath the technology is a human question: how do we find the confidence to build what is next?”

2. Friction is where good ideas go quiet
Time: 2 minutes

Slide copy:

Friction is not a lack of talent. It is the distance between intention and action.

Visual: An idea behind doors labelled time, access, language, process and confidence.

Talk track:
“Think about the last idea that stayed an idea. Maybe you could see the problem clearly, but not the technical path. Maybe you had the skills, but not the time, permission or confidence. Most ideas do not disappear because people lack imagination. They disappear in the friction between intention and action.”

Audience moment: Ask for a quiet show of hands: “Who has an idea waiting for its first version?”

3. Confidence is not certainty
Time: 1.5 minutes

Slide copy:

Confidence is not certainty. Confidence is a next step plus feedback.

Visual: Try -> See -> Learn -> Try again

Talk track:
“We are often told that confidence comes before action. But confidence is often built the other way around. A draft gives us something to question. A prototype gives us something to improve. Confidence is trusting yourself to take a sensible next step, look at what happened and learn.”

4. AI is not the hero. It is the bridge
Time: 2 minutes

Slide copy:

AI can help ideas cross the gap between intention and a first version.

Talk track:
“When I say GitHub AI, I am not asking everyone here to learn a new programming language. I mean AI capabilities, including GitHub Copilot and agentic experiences, that help people explore a problem, make a plan, draft an artefact, explain a decision, create tests or documentation, and respond to feedback.”

“For a non-coder, that artefact might be a journey map, requirements list, content structure, test scenario or prototype. The useful mental model is not ‘oracle’. It is ‘fast first-draft partner’.”

5. The agentic SDLC: from intent to learning
Time: 3 minutes

Slide copy:

Intent -> Plan -> Build -> Test -> Review -> Secure -> Ship -> Learn

Centre label:
People set direction

Talk track:
“Software development has always been more than writing code. It starts with understanding a need, deciding what good looks like, designing, building, testing, reviewing, protecting people and data, releasing and learning.”

“An agentic lifecycle adds AI systems that can take on bounded, multi-step work. An agent can inspect approved context, make a plan, use approved tools, produce an output, check its work and report back.”

“The word ‘agentic’ does not mean hands-off. It means AI can help carry work across several steps while people remain responsible for direction, constraints, decisions and approval.”

6. Agents are teammates with a job, not magic
Time: 2 minutes

Slide copy:

A useful agent has a clear job, a bounded role and a way to show its work.

Visual: Four role cards:

Researcher
Planner
Builder
Tester/reviewer
Talk track:
“An AI agent is closer to a specialist teammate than a magic button. One agent might research approved context. Another might turn a goal into a plan. Another might create a first version. A testing agent might ask what happens for a user with a slower connection or different needs.”

“The important words are ‘defined’ and ‘allowed’. Good agents have limited responsibilities, limited access and a way to show what they did.”

7. The human remains the source of meaning
Time: 2.5 minutes

Slide copy:

AI can propose. Humans decide what matters.

Visual: Three words around a person:

Purpose
Boundaries
Judgment
Talk track:
“There are three questions AI cannot answer on our behalf. What is the purpose? What are the boundaries? And is this actually good enough?”

“This is where diverse teams matter. The person who notices the accessibility issue. The person who sees who is missing from the data. The person who knows what happens in the real world. AI can make it easier to produce more. It makes human judgment more important, not less.”

8. A story: the idea that became tangible
Time: 3 minutes

Slide copy:

The breakthrough was not the finished product. It was having something to react to.

Story structure:
A community organiser wants to help people new to the industry find mentors. She has the insight and relationships, but no product team or weeks to build.

Talk track:
“She starts with a conversation: ‘Help me map the journey from wanting a mentor to having a useful first conversation.’ AI helps create a journey map, questions and a rough first version.”

“Then real people react. One says the language feels intimidating. Another needs a mobile-friendly option. Someone else points out that the idea assumes people already have a professional network.”

“The first version did not prove the idea was finished. It made learning possible. It turned private confidence into shared progress.”

Best practice: replace this illustrative story with a true story from Michelle's work, community or customer experience.

9. Flow is a team sport
Time: 2 minutes

Slide copy:

Every role can improve the build.

Visual:
Problem framer | Designer | Developer | Domain expert | User researcher | Security thinker | Communicator | Tester

Talk track:
“Flow is not one person moving faster in isolation. It is a team making the next useful handoff easier.”

“You do not have to write code to contribute. You can define the problem, provide real examples, describe what good looks like, test the experience, check the language, notice who has been excluded or ask the question that prevents a team from building the wrong thing.”

10. Build bravely. Build with guardrails
Time: 2 minutes

Slide copy:

Speed without trust is not flow.

Four guardrails:

Start small
Keep human approval
Protect data
Make quality visible
Talk track:
“Responsible building is not the opposite of bold building. It is what allows bold building to last.”

“Use reversible experiments. Keep human approval where the impact is high. Protect sensitive information. Test for security, privacy, accessibility, bias and reliability. The question is not only ‘Can AI do this?’ It is also ‘Should it? Under what conditions? And who reviews the result?’”

11. Three moves before next week
Time: 2 minutes

Slide copy:

Name the friction
Invite a partner
Make one artefact
Talk track:
“First, name the friction in one sentence: ‘People cannot...’ or ‘We lose time when...’”

“Second, invite a partner - a colleague, customer, community member, developer, designer or AI partner with a clearly bounded task.”

“Third, make one artefact: a journey map, checklist, mock-up, draft policy, set of test questions or small prototype. Make it visible enough that another person can respond.”

Audience moment:
Ask the room to silently complete:

“The friction I want to reduce is...”
“The first artefact I could make is...”
12. The future is not something we wait for
Time: 2 minutes

Slide copy:

The confidence to build what's next is built along the way.

Closing talk track:
“You do not need to know everything before you begin. You do not need to become someone else before your idea deserves a first version. You do not need to build alone.”

“Bring the question. Bring the context. Bring the care. Use AI to reduce the blank page, and use your judgment to decide what belongs on it.”

“The confidence to build what is next is not the price of admission. It is what we build, together, along the way.”
